How Search Works — Crawling, Indexing, Ranking

Updated 2026-08-05 · by Connor Enge

On this page

  • Crawling
  • Indexing
  • Ranking
  • Serving
  • Why this matters

Every SEO decision is easier when you know the four stages a page passes through before it can rank.

Crawling

Google uses automated programs called crawlers (Googlebot) to discover pages by following links and reading sitemaps. If a page isn't linked from anywhere and isn't in your sitemap, it may never be found. Your job at this stage: make important pages discoverable and don't waste crawl budget on junk.

Indexing

After crawling, Google tries to understand the page — its topic, its content, its media — and stores it in the index. Pages can be crawled but not indexed if they're thin, duplicate, blocked by noindex, or judged low value. If it isn't indexed, it cannot rank.

Ranking

When someone searches, Google's systems select and order results from the index based on hundreds of signals: relevance to the query, content quality, usability (including Core Web Vitals), and the site's overall reputation. There is no single "ranking button" — you improve the whole picture.

Serving

Finally Google assembles the results page, often personalized by language, location, and device. The same query can look different for two people, which is why rank tracking is directional, not absolute.

Why this matters

Most SEO problems are really stage problems: "we're not ranking" is usually "we're not indexed," which is usually "we're not crawlable" or "the content isn't strong enough." Diagnose in that order.
