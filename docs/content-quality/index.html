Content Quality, Helpfulness & E-E-A-T

Updated 2026-08-05 · by Connor Enge

On this page

  • The helpful-content test
  • E-E-A-T
  • Practical moves

Technical SEO gets you in the door; content is why you rank and stay ranked. Google's guidance is explicit: create content for people, not to manipulate rankings.

The helpful-content test

Google publishes self-assessment questions. The strongest pages can answer "yes" to:

  • Does it provide original information, reporting, research, or analysis?
  • Is it a substantial, complete treatment of the topic?
  • Does it offer insight beyond the obvious — not just a rewrite of other pages?
  • Would someone feel they'd learned enough to stop searching?
  • Is it written by someone with demonstrable expertise or first-hand experience?

E-E-A-T

Google's quality raters look for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust. It's not a direct score, but it describes what good pages have:

  • Experience — first-hand use or knowledge (you've actually done the thing).
  • Expertise — depth and accuracy for the topic.
  • Authoritativeness — recognized as a go-to source by others.
  • Trust — accurate, safe, transparent; clear authorship and sourcing. Trust is the most important member of the family.

Practical moves

  • Put a real author with credentials on the page.
  • Cite primary sources; keep facts current and dated.
  • Cover the topic completely — answer the follow-up questions too.
  • Remove or improve thin, outdated, or duplicative pages instead of letting them drag the site down.
