Local SEO improves visibility for businesses that serve a specific geographic area, whether through a storefront or by traveling to customers. When people search on Google Search or Google Maps for nearby options, results often appear in a local pack or map interface that sits apart from standard web results. The goal is to align business information with what searchers need so the system can surface the right options.

## The three main signals in local rankings
Google states that local results rely primarily on three factors. Relevance measures how closely a Business Profile matches the terms in a search. Distance reflects how near each business is to the searcher's location or to an estimated location when none is shared. Prominence assesses how well-known a business appears based on information gathered across the web, such as links from other sites and the volume and tone of reviews.

These signals cannot be purchased or directly requested. Algorithm details remain internal, so the practical approach focuses on the controllable elements within each factor.

## Google Business Profile as the core record
A complete and verified Google Business Profile forms the foundation for local visibility. Verification confirms authorization to represent the business and increases the chance that information will appear in results. Profiles with full, accurate details are more likely to be shown and less likely to be filtered out.

Recommended information includes the full address when customers visit in person, regular and special hours, the primary business category, and attributes such as parking or accessibility options. Adding photos and videos provides visual context for what the business offers. In eligible regions, retail businesses can also list in-store products that may surface in local results.

Responding to reviews demonstrates attention to feedback. Positive reviews paired with helpful replies can strengthen the profile's standing in local results.

## Keeping name, address, and phone consistent
Name, address, and phone number data must match exactly across the Business Profile, the business website, and third-party directories. Inconsistencies make it harder for both users and Google's systems to connect signals reliably to one business. Regular checks help catch discrepancies that arise from directory updates or data aggregators.

## Reviews and prominence
Reviews contribute to prominence through their quantity and ratings. The process begins with asking satisfied customers for honest feedback. Replies to reviews show engagement without altering the original rating. Policies prohibit fake, purchased, or self-written reviews because they violate guidelines and reduce long-term trust.

## On-site practices for location-based pages
When a business operates multiple locations, each site needs its own dedicated page that includes the correct address, hours, and content specific to that place. Duplicated or thin pages reduce usefulness. City or region names belong in titles, headings, and text only where they reflect actual operations and do not crowd the copy.

A contact page should present the NAP information clearly, and an embedded map can help users confirm the location. Internal links from other pages on the site make location and service content easier to reach.

## Structured data for local businesses
LocalBusiness structured data on a page that already displays business details helps Google interpret the information. Use the most specific subtype available, such as Restaurant or HealthClub, and list multiple applicable types as an array when needed.

Required properties are `name` and `address`, with the address expressed as a PostalAddress that includes streetAddress, addressLocality, addressRegion, postalCode, and addressCountry whenever possible. Recommended properties cover `geo` coordinates, `telephone`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `priceRange`, and `url`.

`aggregateRating` and `review` properties are intended only for sites that collect reviews about other businesses. Adding them to a page about the business itself is not appropriate. Validate markup with the Rich Results Test tool and ensure the data matches what appears on the page.

## Measuring results
Performance data from the Business Profile shows how people discover the listing, request directions, place calls, or visit the website. Google Search Console provides additional detail on how the site's location pages perform in search. Focus on outcomes such as calls or visits rather than isolated ranking positions.

## Issues that commonly reduce visibility
Profiles that remain unverified or incomplete often fail to appear. Incorrect or mismatched NAP data creates confusion across sources. Selecting the wrong primary category limits relevance matching. Multi-location sites suffer when pages lack distinct, substantive content. Keyword repetition in business names or other fields can trigger filtering. Fake or incentivized reviews and self-review structured data violate policy. Ignoring incoming reviews removes an opportunity to strengthen prominence signals.

Maintaining accurate, consistent information across the Business Profile and supporting web presence addresses the factors Google uses while staying within stated guidelines.
