Keyword research is not about stuffing phrases — it's about discovering **the questions your audience is actually asking** and the intent behind them.

## Start with intent, not volume
Every query has an intent. Match the page type to it:

- **Informational** ("how does seo work") → guides, explainers.
- **Commercial** ("best seo tools") → comparisons, reviews.
- **Transactional** ("hire seo agency") → service/product pages.
- **Navigational** ("search console login") → a specific destination.

Ranking a service page for an informational query — or vice versa — fails no matter how good the page is.

## Find the terms
- Use **Google Search Console** to see queries you already get impressions for (your fastest wins).
- Mine **autocomplete**, **People Also Ask**, and **related searches** on the results page.
- Read the questions real customers ask in support tickets, sales calls, and forums.
- Note **long-tail** phrases (3+ words): lower volume, higher intent, easier to win.

## Map keywords to pages
Build a simple map: one **primary intent per page**. Group closely related terms onto the same page (they're the same intent); split genuinely different intents onto separate pages. This prevents two of your own pages from competing (keyword cannibalization).

## Judge the difficulty honestly
Look at who currently ranks. If page one is dominated by huge, established brands, target a **more specific** version of the query where you can genuinely be the best answer. Then earn your way up.

## FAQ

### How many keywords should one page target?
One primary intent per page. Group closely related phrasings of the same question onto that page — they are the same search — but give a genuinely different intent its own page. Trying to rank a single page for several unrelated queries usually means it ranks well for none.

### What's the difference between search volume and search intent?
Volume is how many people search a term; intent is what they actually want when they do. Chasing volume without matching intent produces traffic that bounces. A lower-volume query you can answer perfectly is worth more than a high-volume one your page only half-satisfies.

### How do I find low-competition keywords?
Look for long-tail queries of three or more words, question phrasings from People Also Ask, and the specific language customers use in support tickets and sales calls. Then check who currently ranks — if page one is all major brands, pick a more specific angle where your page can honestly be the best result.

### What is keyword cannibalization and how do I avoid it?
Cannibalization is when two of your own pages compete for the same query, splitting signals so neither ranks well. Avoid it by mapping one primary intent per page and consolidating overlapping pages — merge them, or point the weaker one at the stronger with a canonical or redirect.
