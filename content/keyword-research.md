Keyword research is not about stuffing phrases — it's about discovering **the questions your audience is actually asking** and the intent behind them.

## Start with intent, not volume
Every query has an intent. Match the page type to it:

- **Informational** ("how does seo work") → guides, explainers.
- **Commercial** ("best seo tools") → comparisons, reviews.
- **Transactional** ("hire seo agency") → service/product pages.
- **Navigational** ("search console login") → a specific destination.

Ranking a service page for an informational query — or vice versa — fails no matter how good the page is.

## Find the terms
- Use **Google Search Console** to see queries you already get impressions for (your fastest wins).
- Mine **autocomplete**, **People Also Ask**, and **related searches** on the results page.
- Read the questions real customers ask in support tickets, sales calls, and forums.
- Note **long-tail** phrases (3+ words): lower volume, higher intent, easier to win.

## Map keywords to pages
Build a simple map: one **primary intent per page**. Group closely related terms onto the same page (they're the same intent); split genuinely different intents onto separate pages. This prevents two of your own pages from competing (keyword cannibalization).

## Judge the difficulty honestly
Look at who currently ranks. If page one is dominated by huge, established brands, target a **more specific** version of the query where you can genuinely be the best answer. Then earn your way up.
